pH level

Pure water. Natural pH.

Reverse osmosis removes dissolved solids that act as buffers in tap water. The result is water with a naturally lower pH — consistent with the chemistry of pure H₂O and not a sign of anything wrong with the water.

Pureau pH (at factory)
7.1 – 7.4

Pureau leaves the facility at pH 7.1–7.4. Without dissolved minerals to act as buffers, the pH drops as the water absorbs CO₂ from the air after opening — the same process that occurs with rainwater.

Tap water pH
7.5 – 8.5

Tap water pH is elevated by dissolved minerals, chlorine compounds and, in some states, deliberate pH adjustment for pipe corrosion control.

Testing
ALS certified

Pureau pH is measured as part of the daily Australian Laboratory Services testing protocol across every production run.

Why does the pH of pure water change?

Pureau leaves the facility at pH 7.1–7.4 — measured by our laboratory across every production run. Without dissolved minerals to act as buffers, the water absorbs CO₂ from the air after opening, which lowers the pH over time. This is the same natural process that gives rainwater its pH of 5.0–5.8.

The World Health Organisation notes that there is no health-based guideline value for pH in drinking water and that pH outside the 6.5–8.5 range does not pose a direct health risk. The pH change in Pureau is normal chemistry, not a quality problem.
