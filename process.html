The Pureau process

Three stages. No chemicals.

Every drop of Pureau passes through three independently verified stages before it leaves our facility. This is what 100% pure water actually takes.

Tap water in

First stage

Filtration

Carbon filtration and inorganic media remove large contaminants and inorganic materials from the incoming water. This stage conditions the water for the high-pressure reverse osmosis step that follows.

Cl
Chlorine and chloramines
Added to tap water as disinfectant — removed at Stage 1.
Fe
Iron and sediment
Dissolved metals and particulate matter from ageing pipes.
NO
Nitrates
Common in agricultural catchments, removed via specialised carbon media.
Carbon & inorganic media
Decades of refinement
Second stage

Reverse osmosis

Water is forced under high pressure through semi-permeable membranes. Dissolved solids, fluoride, sodium, heavy metals and most remaining contaminants cannot pass through the membrane and are rejected.

F
Fluoride
State-added fluoride is rejected by the RO membrane.
Na
Sodium
Dissolved sodium salts are blocked — not detected in finished product.
PF
PFAS
"Forever chemicals" found in some Australian catchments — removed by RO.
High-pressure membrane
Sub-micron rejection
Third stage

Biological filtration

The final security measure. Biological filters remove any remaining substances down to 0.2 microns — smaller than any known bacteria or virus. This is the stage that separates a Pureau plant from every home filter on the market.

BK
Bacteria
0.2-micron rated filters block all known bacterial organisms.
VS
Viruses and microbes
Sub-micron filtration removes organisms that RO alone may miss.
ALS
Daily ALS verification
Samples sent to Australian Laboratory Services every single production run.
0.2 micron rated
ALS certified daily

Independently verified. Every run.

Pureau sends samples to Australian Laboratory Services (ALS) daily across every production run at our Erskine Park facility. Results are reviewed and signed off before any product leaves the site.

Home filtration systems have no equivalent verification. They produce water of variable quality — depending on when filters were last replaced, the condition of the storage bladder, and the source water temperature.
